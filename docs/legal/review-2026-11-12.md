## 2. Provider-dependent: confirm with Stables

1. **Third-party beneficiaries.** The terms allow payouts "to a named beneficiary you are
   allowed to pay". Stables has to confirm that third-party payouts are allowed per corridor.
   If they are not, change this to "your own bank account".
2. **Refunds of failed payouts.** The terms say Stables makes the refunds under its own terms.
   The refund route (to which wallet, in which asset, after what time) is not confirmed.
3. **Limits.** The maximum amount follows Stables' production limits. The pages refer to "the
   limits shown in the app" and give no number. That stays correct whatever Stables sets.
4. **Countries.** The live corridor list depends on Stables' production approval.

## 3. For the lawyer: questions the pages cannot settle

1. **UK cryptoasset financial promotions (FCA, since October 2023).** The public site and
   the Trade page describe buying and selling cryptoassets to UK visitors. Is this a
   financial promotion, and does it need an authorised approver, risk warnings in the
   prescribed form, a 24-hour cooling-off period or appropriateness checks? This matters
   most for **Trade**.
2. **MLR registration.** Does non-custodial software that routes users to a licensed payout
   partner need its own FCA cryptoasset registration under the Money Laundering Regulations?
3. **Consumer rights.** Is a cancellation-rights statement needed for a digital service under
   the Consumer Contracts Regulations? Is a complaints-handling timeline needed?
4. **Retention.** The privacy policy says payment records are kept "typically six years".
   The MLR minimum is five years after the relationship ends. Confirm which period applies
   and who keeps the records (LamportPay or Stables).
5. **ICO data protection fee.** Confirm that Lamport Pay Ltd is registered and has paid it.
6. **International transfers.** Hyperliquid, Jupiter and the RPC providers process data
   outside the UK. Confirm the safeguards wording, and whether Hyperliquid is a recipient
   or an independent controller.
7. **Liability cap.** The terms exclude indirect loss but set no monetary cap. Is a cap
   (for example, fees paid in the last 12 months) wanted, and is it enforceable against
   consumers?
8. **Under-18s.** The terms say 18+. The privacy policy could say the same, with what
   happens if a minor's data is found.
